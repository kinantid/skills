# Managing Up

**Source**: Wes Kao, "15 principles for managing up"

## The core idea

Managing up isn't sucking up. It's making your manager's (or any senior stakeholder's) job easier so they can make yours easier in return. Done well, it buys you autonomy, trust, and the best projects. Done poorly (or not at all), it caps your career.

## The principles this skill leans on

A selected subset — the managing-up moves that most affect *written* comms — reworded and reordered for this skill. This is not Kao's full list; for all 15 in her own words, read the original: [Wes Kao, "15 principles for managing up"](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/15-principles-for-managing-up).

### 1. Make their job easier
Every interaction should lower their cognitive load or unblock something. If you're adding work to their plate, compensate with disproportionate value.

### 2. State your recommendation, then the reasoning
BLUF: bottom line up front. *"Recommending we push the launch. Reasoning below."* Not: *"Here are five considerations, let's see where we land."*

### 3. Bring solutions, not just problems
When flagging an issue, bring your recommendation. *"X is broken. I think we should do Y; here's why; want me to run with that?"* Not: *"X is broken, what do you want to do?"*

### 4. Make asks specific
*"Can you approve X by Thursday?"* not *"Let me know your thoughts when you get a chance."*

### 5. Don't over-apologise
Senior people hate apology inflation. Apologise once, fix it, move on. *"Missed the deadline. Here's what I'm doing to fix it."* Not five paragraphs of remorse.

### 6. Protect their time
Don't book 30 min when you need 5. Don't Slack eight messages when one would do. Don't ask questions you can google. Their calendar is a resource; you're a steward of it.

## How this skill uses the principles

When refining a message going **upward** (CEO, manager, exec, board), these apply strictly:

- Make their job easier → flag the decision you want, don't leave it ambiguous.
- Recommendation first (BLUF) → always.
- Solutions not problems → if flagging an issue, include your recommendation.
- Specific asks → always.
- Don't over-apologise → strip "sorry to bother", "just wanted to flag", "if you have a sec".
- Protect their time → strictest possible concision pass.

## Before / after

**Before (bad managing up):**
> Hi [CEO], hope your week is going well! I'm really sorry to bother you but I was wondering if you might have some time to talk about the Q3 roadmap. I know you're super busy so no rush at all, just whenever works! I have some thoughts I wanted to share but I'm not sure what the best approach is. Let me know what you think when you get a sec. Thanks so much!!

Violates: over-apologising, a vague ask, no clear recommendation, no respect for their time.

**After:**
> [CEO], want to lock Q3 roadmap this week. Recommending onboarding revamp as P0 (memo attached, 2-min read). 15 min Wed or Thu to sign off?

One clear ask, one clear recommendation, one clear time box.

## Common failure mode

Confusing managing up with performing. If you're sending constant updates about how hard you're working, that's not managing up. That's optics. Managing up is invisible when it works; the manager just notices their life got easier.
