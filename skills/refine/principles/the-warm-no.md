# The Warm No (declining while keeping the relationship)

**Source**: composed playbook. The "enthusiastic yes" inversion is Wes Kao; the "one recallable specific" close is Alison Green (Ask a Manager). Surfaced across the Product Engineer rejection batch (Jun 2026).

## The core idea

The inverse of `enthusiastic-yes.md`. When the answer is no, the goal isn't just to deliver it cleanly, it's to leave the relationship intact and warm enough that the person would happily talk to you again. Most "warm" closes fail in the opposite direction: they get sappy, overcommit, or quietly push the work back onto the person you just declined.

This applies well beyond hiring rejections: declining a customer feature request, passing on a vendor, saying no to a partnership, turning down an intro.

## The three moves

### 1. Put the next step on yourself, not them

When you want to keep a relationship warm, carry the action yourself.

- Good: *"I'll reach out when we reopen the role."*
- Weak: *"Please apply again when we're hiring."*

The onus shouldn't sit on the person you just said no to. Same move for any "let's stay in touch": name what *you* will do, or it reads as a polite brush-off. A next step the other person has to initiate is not a warm close, it's homework.

### 2. Bounded offers keep warmth from becoming overcommitment

A genuine, useful offer beats a vague open-ended one, and protects you from promising something you don't mean.

- Good: *"Happy to send through a few resources, just shout."*
- Overcommitted: *"Let me know if you ever want to chat about your career, mentorship, anything at all."*

Name the specific, low-commitment thing. Don't imply standing mentorship or an open door you won't actually hold open. A bounded offer you'll honour is warmer than a grand one you won't.

### 3. Personalise the close with one recallable specific

One genuine detail makes a warm close land as human rather than templated: their App Store goal, the Chrome-store approval they're waiting on, the side project they mentioned, the coffee they said they were about to grab.

One, not three. A single specific reads as "I was paying attention." A pile of them reads as a CRM record.

- Good: *"Good luck with the App Store submission, hope the review goes smoothly."*
- Templated: *"Wishing you all the best in your future endeavours."*

## How this interacts with the rest of the skill

- **Receipts still apply.** The one recallable specific has to be true and correctly remembered. A detail you've garbled is worse than none. See `principles/accurate-confidence.md` (receipts before you credit).
- **No performative warmth.** Don't announce the warmth ("I genuinely wish you the best", "honestly it was a pleasure"). State the specific and stop, same as the voice defaults in SKILL.md.
- **Strategy, not self-expression.** The close serves the relationship, not your need to feel like the good guy about the no.

## Common failure mode

Softening the no itself to compensate for the warm close. The decline should be clear and kind; the warmth lives in the close, not in hedging the actual answer. A muddy no with a warm sign-off leaves the person unsure whether they were even rejected. Be clear about the no, warm about the person.
