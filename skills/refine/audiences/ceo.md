# Audience: CEO / Exec / Board

**Strictness**: strict (maximum)

## Who this is for

Writing upward to the CEO at your company, other C-suite, board members, or anyone at a comparable level of remove and time-constraint.

## Their reading context

- Reading between meetings, often on mobile.
- Context-switching every 5–15 minutes.
- Simultaneously tracking 20+ threads across the business.
- Pattern-matching fast, will form a judgment from the first sentence.
- Low patience for process, narrative arc, or demonstrations of effort.

## Default principles to apply (in order)

1. **BLUF** (bottom line up front): first sentence = the point, ask, or recommendation. Non-negotiable.
2. **Minimum Viable Backstory**: cut all setup. If they need it, they'll ask.
3. **One hero number**: lead with the single most important quantitative fact.
4. **MOO** (most obvious objection): address the first objection they'll have in the same breath. They *will* have one.
5. **Finesse**: when flagging a risk or bad news, frame the true fact as a managed thing — "here's the risk and here's how it's handled" lands very differently from "this might be a problem". Never spin; just lead with the defensible positive truth. See `principles/finesse.md`.
6. **Right amount of context**: minimum viable; offer links for more.
7. **Managing up**: strip apology, hedging, process talk.

## What they want to see

- The point, stated plainly.
- A recommendation, not a menu.
- A clear ask, by when, from whom.
- Calibrated confidence (honest about uncertainty, not hedging).
- An acknowledgement of the obvious risk or objection.
- A next step.

## What they don't want to see

- "Hope this finds you well" / "Quick check-in" / "Just wanted to flag"
- Paragraphs of background before the point.
- A long list of options without a recommendation.
- Emotional framing ("I'm really excited about this").
- Multiple asks in one message (pick the most important; save the rest).
- Apology inflation ("so sorry to bother").
- Excessive "FYI": if it's not actionable, don't send it.

## Structural template (for any upward comm)

```
[Recommendation / decision / ask: one sentence]

[One hero number or fact supporting it]

[MOO + your response, one sentence]

[Ask + by-when]
```

Anything longer needs to earn the length.

## Slack to CEO (example)

> Recommending we push the launch to 20 May (from 13 May). Eng needs one more week for the billing integration; pushing it protects revenue ($180k deal tied to billing working). Will brief sales today. OK with this unless I hear back by EOD.

One sentence = recommendation. One hero = the $180k. MOO = "we shouldn't delay" is addressed with revenue risk. Ask = implicit approval, with a clear default if silent.

## Email to CEO (example)

**Subject**: Q2 onboarding: on track, one decision needed

> Onboarding redesign is on track to ship 3 May. Early prototype data shows activation at **38%** (vs. 28% baseline, best number we've ever seen at this stage).
>
> One decision needed: we can ship with the full flow or split into two phases. I recommend shipping full (reasons below).
>
> **Full ship pros**: cleaner data read, 40% of the lift comes from step 3 which is only in the full flow.
> **Split pros**: 1-week earlier; reduces eng risk.
> **My read**: worth the week. Data read is more valuable than a 1-week head start.
>
> Sign-off by Friday? Default to full if no response.

## Strictness calibration

**Apply strictly at all times:**
- BLUF
- Concision (aggressive cut pass)
- Specific ask with deadline
- MOO addressed
- No hedging ("just", "maybe", "wondering")

**Apply lightly / case by case:**
- Warmth (a bit of warmth is fine; lots of warmth reads as time-wasting)
- Structural formatting (OK for longer pieces; unnecessary for a 2-sentence Slack)

## What to push back on

If a draft to the CEO has any of these, rewrite freely:

- Buried lede (recommendation in paragraph 2 or later)
- No recommendation (presenting options without choosing)
- No ask or deadline
- Hedges ("I think maybe we could possibly consider")
- Apology inflation
- Long setup before the point
- Multiple asks jammed together

## Common mistakes for PMs writing upward

1. **Over-explaining the analysis.** The CEO trusts your analysis is there; they want the conclusion.
2. **Under-claiming wins.** A 34% lift stated flatly reads as small; a 34% lift stated clearly (with context that it's 3x the baseline) reads as large.
3. **Soft-ending.** "Let me know your thoughts" is an anti-close. "Approve by Friday?" is a close.
4. **Asking permission instead of proposing direction.** *"What do you think we should do?"* is a junior move. *"I recommend X, unless you see a reason to reconsider"* is a senior one.

## When to break the rules

- **Personal / relationship moments** (congratulations, condolences, team dynamics): these are self-expression-appropriate. Warmth is the content.
- **Strategic upward disagreement**: sometimes you need more context to dissent well. Still lead with the disagreement; just give yourself room to justify.
- **Receiving bad news delivery**: if the CEO is upset, don't be cold on top of it. Measured, direct, not clipped.
