# Signposting (reduce cognitive load)

**Source**: Wes Kao, "Sign-posting: how to reduce cognitive load"

## The core idea

Signposting is giving the reader a map before you walk them through the terrain. You tell them what's coming, where they are, and where they're going. Done well, it lets the reader relax: they know what to expect and can focus on the content instead of trying to reverse-engineer the structure.

Three kinds of signposts:

1. **Meta-signposts**: tell the reader the shape of what's coming. *"I'll cover three things: A, B, and C."*
2. **Transition signposts**: tell the reader where they are in that shape. *"That's the context. Now the ask."*
3. **Emphasis signposts**: tell the reader what to pay attention to. *"The most important number here is X."*

## Why it matters

Without signposts, readers have to do the work of parsing structure themselves. That costs them attention, attention they'd otherwise spend on your actual point. Busy readers (execs, customers) don't have the patience; they'll skim or bail.

Signposts also make skimmable content possible. A reader who sees "The ask:" as a bolded sub-header can jump straight to it. That's a gift.

## How to apply it

For anything longer than three sentences, consider:

- A one-line lede that states the point AND previews the structure.
  - *"Recommending we move the launch to June: three reasons below, plus one risk."*
- Inline transitions when shifting topics.
  - *"That's the problem. Here's what I want to do about it."*
- Bolded micro-headers in emails/memos: "Context", "Recommendation", "Risks", "Ask".
- Numbered lists when the order matters ("first, second, third"). Bulleted when it doesn't.

For Slack, signposts are usually just 2–4 words at the start of each message or thought: *"Quick update:"*, *"One ask:"*, *"Context:"*.

## Before / after

**Before (no signposts):**
> We've been thinking about the pricing model. There's a lot of variability in how customers are using the product now which makes usage-based tricky but tiered has its own issues. Sam ran some numbers and it looks like tiered would leave about $200k on the table annually vs usage but it's simpler to execute. The eng team said they could do either one in 4 weeks though usage is more complex. Thoughts?

**After (with signposts):**
> **Pricing model: recommending tiered.** Context, tradeoffs, and one ask below.
>
> **Context**: customer usage is varying a lot, which makes both options viable.
>
> **The tradeoff**: tiered leaves ~$200k/yr on the table vs usage, but it's simpler to execute (4 weeks either way; usage is more complex).
>
> **Ask**: sign-off by Friday so we can brief sales.

Same information. Second version is instantly skimmable and the reader knows exactly what's coming.

## Common failure mode

Signposts without substance. "I want to share three things" followed by fluff. If the signpost is promising more than the content delivers, cut the signpost. It'll just annoy the reader.

## Rule of thumb

If someone had to read only the first and last sentences, would they get the gist? If not, the signposting is too thin.
