# Minimum Viable Backstory (start right before you get eaten by the bear)

**Source**: Wes Kao, "Start right before you get eaten by the bear"

## The core idea

Most people start a story too early. They explain the setup, the cast of characters, the weather, the history, and by the time they get to the actual point, the listener has checked out.

The fix: **start right before you get eaten by the bear.** Begin at the moment of highest stakes or clearest relevance. Strip everything that happened before that moment down to one or two sentences of context: just enough to make the rest make sense. That's the Minimum Viable Backstory (MVB).

## Why it matters

Readers (especially execs, CEOs, customers) give you maybe 10 seconds to earn more. If the first sentence isn't doing load-bearing work (either the stakes, the ask, or the main insight), you've already lost them. The backstory can come later, if needed. Often it's not needed at all.

## How to apply it

1. Identify the bear. What's the actual dramatic or decision moment in what you're writing? The thing the reader cares about? That's the open.
2. Ask: does the reader need this setup to understand the point? If yes, compress it to one sentence. If no, cut it entirely.
3. Trust the reader to ask follow-up questions if they need more context.

## Before / after

**Before (too much backstory):**
> Last quarter we kicked off a new initiative around onboarding. You might remember that we'd been seeing drop-off at the 7-day mark and Sam proposed a series of in-app prompts. We spent about six weeks building it, with help from design and eng. We shipped it three weeks ago and have been monitoring the data since. The results are in and I wanted to share them with you.

**After (MVB):**
> The onboarding prompts we shipped three weeks ago cut 7-day drop-off by 34%. Full breakdown below.

The "before" spends 80 words getting to the point. The "after" opens with the point and the stakes. If the reader wants the history, they'll ask.

## When NOT to apply

Two edge cases:

- **Relationship-building comms to a peer or friend**: leading with "the bear" can feel cold. "How was your weekend?" is fine.
- **Legal/compliance/safety comms**: sometimes you need the setup because regulatory context changes the meaning of the main point. Rare for most PM work.

## Common failure mode

People think they're starting at the bear but they're actually starting two scenes earlier. Rule of thumb: cut your first paragraph. If the piece still makes sense, that paragraph was backstory.
