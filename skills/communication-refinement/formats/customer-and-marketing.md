# Format: Customer & Marketing Copy

## Why customer / marketing copy is different

External-facing copy is high-stakes in a specific way: you're writing to someone who doesn't owe you attention. Every sentence has to earn the next one. If you lose them, you don't get a recovery window. They just move on.

This isn't internal comms with extra politeness. It's a different discipline:

- No shared context to lean on.
- No relationship buffer.
- Skeptical, skimming readers.
- Competitors one tab away.
- Claims get scrutinised, quoted, and compared.
- Tone, specificity, and proof all compound.

## Two families of external copy

This file covers two related but distinct use cases:

1. **1:1 customer emails**: account management, renewal nudges, support escalations, relationship comms. Warm, specific, personal.
2. **1:many marketing copy**: landing pages, blog posts, outbound emails, social, in-app marketing. Sharper, more proof-led, optimised for skim.

The principles overlap; the register differs.

## Principles that matter most

In rough priority:

1. **Yellow-spot-into-sun**: lead with the proof, not the promise.
2. **Strategy not self-expression**: you're writing to move the reader, not to express the brand.
3. **MOO**: anticipate "this won't work for me" / "this is too expensive" / "I don't trust this."
4. **Concision**: external copy is the most ruthless context for word economy.
5. **Enthusiastic yes**: the "yes" is a click, a reply, a renewal, a purchase.
6. **Specific > adjectives**: "saved 11 hours a week" beats "drove significant time savings."

## The five-second test

External copy has roughly five seconds to earn the next beat. Every time you draft, read it as a skeptical stranger and ask:

1. In the first 5 seconds, what do I understand?
2. Is there a reason to keep reading?
3. Is there a reason to believe what's being claimed?

If any of these fail, rewrite.

## Structural templates

### Template 1: Landing page / section header

```
[Specific proof point or promise: one line]
[Supporting proof: one line, preferably a number or named customer]
[What it is / who it's for: one line]
[CTA: one clear action, plus one optional lower-commitment option]
```

Example (Northwind landing page):
> Meridian cut project admin by 11 hours a week on Northwind.
>
> Project management built for teams like yours.
> Scheduling, reports, documents, all in one place.
>
> [Book a demo]  [Watch the 2-min tour]

### Template 2: Outbound marketing email

```
Subject: [Specific, curiosity-triggering, 6–10 words]

[Specific hook: why are you emailing them, specifically]

[Proof or promise: one or two sentences]

[Soft CTA: low-commitment first step]
```

Example:
> Subject: 3 ways Meridian sped up report turnaround
>
> Saw you're running 4 projects right now. Probably a lot of report traffic.
>
> Meridian cut average report turnaround from 4 days to under 18 hours after switching to Northwind. We built a short case study if useful.
>
> Want me to send it, or prefer a 15-min walkthrough?

### Template 3: Customer 1:1 email (renewal, relationship)

```
[Specific callback to their world]
[What's relevant to them right now]
[Proof / progress since you last spoke]
[Clear low-friction next step + higher-friction option]
[Warm sign-off]
```

Example:
> Chris, your Northwind renewal's coming up on 12 May.
>
> A few things you asked for have shipped since we last spoke: the reporting dashboard is live, and the mobile upload went out last month.
>
> If it'd help, happy to do a 15-min walkthrough of the new stuff before renewal. Or if everything's working and you want to renew as-is, just reply "all good" and I'll handle it.
>
> Thanks,
> [your name]

### Template 4: Customer de-escalation / issue response

```
[What happened: one line, plain English]
[What's true now: resolved / in progress / ETA]
[Root cause: one line]
[What we're doing to prevent recurrence: one line]
[Direct way to reach you if needed]
```

Example:
> Mei, the sync issue you reported this morning is fixed as of 10:40am. Your data should be fully caught up by 11:30.
>
> Root cause: a webhook retry loop on our side after a deploy yesterday. We've added a monitoring alert so this gets caught in minutes next time, not hours.
>
> If anything's still off after 11:30, ping me directly on [your number].
>
> Thanks for flagging,
> [your name]

## Voice for your customers

Your customers are the practitioners and buyers your product serves. They respond to:

- **Direct and competent** over smooth and salesy.
- **Specific** over aspirational.
- **Practitioner-respectful** humour: dry, understated, not forced.
- **Consistent house voice**: match your company's established spelling and locale. Avoid filler (reach out, touch base, circle back).
- **Short**: their time on admin is limited; customers don't want an essay.

Avoid:

- Marketing hype ("game-changing!", "revolutionary!")
- Abstract B2B jargon ("synergies", "leverage", "best-in-class", "enterprise-grade")
- Breathless enthusiasm ("SO excited to share!")
- Overly personal DTC-style chattiness ("hey friend!"). They're not your friend, they're your customer.

## What to lead with (by piece)

| Piece | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Landing page | Specific customer result or clear promise + proof |
| Outbound email | A specific reason you're reaching out (not a generic hook) |
| Case study | The number / outcome. Details after. |
| Product update | The thing the customer can now do (not what's launched) |
| Renewal email | Specific callbacks to this customer's situation |
| Escalation response | The current status / resolution, not apology |
| Feature announcement | What problem it solves, with a concrete example |

## MOOs in customer / marketing copy

Every piece of external copy has predictable reader objections. Address them:

| Objection | How to address |
|---|---|
| "This doesn't apply to my situation" | Name the specific audience (the teams your product serves) and ideally the specific customer type |
| "Sounds too good to be true" | Concrete numbers + named customers |
| "This'll be a hassle to set up" | Name the setup time and support ("live in a day", "we do the migration") |
| "I don't trust the price tag" | Lead with value, not price. Only name price when trust is earned. |
| "I've been burned before by X tool" | Name the thing competitors get wrong (without naming them) |
| "We already have a tool for this" | Name the specific reason your product is different for *their* workflow |

## Before / after

### Landing page headline

**Before:**
> Northwind is a comprehensive, industry-leading platform designed to help teams streamline their operations and improve project outcomes.

**After:**
> Built for teams like yours. Used by Meridian and Acme.
>
> Scheduling, reports, and docs in one place: set up in a day.

### Feature announcement email

**Before:**
> Hi team, we're thrilled to announce the launch of our brand new Reporting Dashboard! This exciting feature has been months in the making and we're so excited to finally share it with you. You'll be able to see all your reports in one place, track their status, and take action faster. Check it out today!

**After:**
> You can now see every active report across all your projects on one screen, and get pinged when anything's overdue.
>
> Reporting Dashboard went live this morning. It's in your main nav. No setup needed if you're already using reports.
>
> Feedback welcome, hit reply.

### Customer check-in

**Before:**
> Hi Chris! Just wanted to touch base and see how things are going over at [company]. We really value our partnership with you and wanted to make sure everything is working smoothly. Please let us know if there's anything we can do to support you or if you have any feedback. Looking forward to hearing from you!

**After:**
> Chris, been a month since the onboarding call. Any friction I should know about?
>
> Also wanted to flag: the reporting dashboard you asked for is live now. Quick 10-min walkthrough this week if useful, or I can just drop a loom.

## What to push back on (when refining external drafts)

- Corporate boilerplate openings ("We're excited to…") → cut, lead with proof or reader-relevance
- Adjectives without numbers ("leading", "comprehensive", "powerful") → demand specifics or cut
- Vague CTAs ("Learn more") → replace with specific action ("See the 2-min demo")
- Off-brand register in customer comms → retune
- Every-sentence-is-a-sales-pitch energy → let some sentences just be useful
- No named customer when one could be used (with consent) → request it
- Disclaimer / caveat before the value prop → move to footer

## Legal / brand guardrails

External copy is the one place where self-censorship matters:

- **Don't claim specific outcomes unless they're defensible.** "Customers typically save 40%" is dangerous without data. "Meridian saved 11 hours/week" is safe because it's a single attributed claim.
- **Customer names require permission**: never publish a customer name in marketing copy without written consent.
- **Competitive claims**: avoid naming competitors directly; write about the problem they don't solve well instead.

If an external draft makes a claim that would be problematic, flag it and suggest a version that preserves the punch without the risk.

## Final check for external copy

- Would a stranger understand the value in 5 seconds?
- Is there a proof point in the first half of the copy?
- Is the CTA specific and low-friction?
- Does it sound like a human (and specifically, one of your company's people)?
- Has every corporate filler word been cut?
- Is there a specific number or customer name?
- If I'm the customer, would this make me want to reply / click / renew?

If all yes → ship. If any no → revise.
